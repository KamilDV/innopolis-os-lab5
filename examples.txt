# Preliminary examples

`example_pipe.c` creates an unnamed pipe, forks, waits for the child to confirm that
its read end is open, sends the command-line message, and receives its length
through a second pipe. `example_pipe.sh` runs it ten times.

`example_threads.c` creates and joins two threads, so both IDs are printed before exit.
`example_race.c` demonstrates an unsynchronized read-modify-write of `counter`; that
statement is the critical section. Both threads may read the same old value
and lose one increment. The unsynchronized access is a C data race, so its
result is not guaranteed.

`example_join.c` creates one worker at a time and joins it before creating the next.
This serialization keeps the critical sections from overlapping. Joining two
threads only after creating both does **not** protect a shared counter.

`example_mutex.c` runs 1000 threads with 1000 increments each, first without and then
with a mutex. The first count is undefined and may differ from 1,000,000;
the mutex-protected count should be exactly 1,000,000.
